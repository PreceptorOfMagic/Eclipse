# Supporting Eclipse/Umbra

Buy Me a Coffee is the project's single support route. The public [`PreceptorOfMagic`](https://buymeacoffee.com/preceptorofmagic) page is live and connected to an Australian Stripe Standard account; GitHub and the project site now point to that same destination.

This file records the working setup and the Australian issues checked on 1 October 2026. It is project policy and research, not personal legal or tax advice.

## Selected model

- Use one **Buy Me a Coffee** page connected to an Australian **Stripe Standard Connect** account. Australia is on Buy Me a Coffee's supported-payout list.
- The legal recipient is the individual maintainer unless that structure is deliberately changed. Eclipse and Umbra remain application/project names, not separate legal recipients.
- Offer reward-free one-time support and Buy Me a Coffee's optional **Monthly Support**. Monthly Support is recurring but is not a membership and carries no perks. Do not offer memberships, Shop items, commissions, supporter-only content, rewards, paid feature priority, early access or influence over technical decisions.
- Present the payment as a voluntary tip to the maintainer for work already published. It is not payment for goods or services, is not earmarked for a promised feature and creates no obligation to continue development.
- Keep the software, source, documentation, releases and public issue trackers equally available to everyone.

Buy Me a Coffee publishes a 5% platform fee, 2.9% + USD 0.30 processing, a 0.5% payout fee and, where applicable, another 1% for international transactions or 0.5% for recurring payments. Its published schedule does not match Stripe's ordinary Australian Standard pricing, so the two schedules must not be added together. Until a real transaction shows the connected account's actual treatment, disclose Buy Me a Coffee's schedule and then preserve the evidenced gross-to-net result. The live page uses USD as its base currency and can show a local AUD conversion; keep the original currency, conversion and bank-payout evidence for every transaction.

The first payout is reviewed manually. Buy Me a Coffee currently states a USD 10 minimum and says the first payout can take roughly 7–14 days, depending on review, location and bank. Because that review begins only after support has been received, it is a post-activation operational check rather than a prerequisite for linking the page.

## ABN conclusion

There is **no blanket Australian rule requiring an ABN merely to receive Buy Me a Coffee payments**, and Buy Me a Coffee does not publish one. The Australian Government also says an ABN is not compulsory for every business and that a hobby is not entitled to one.

Stripe's current Connect requirements vary with the platform configuration, capabilities, entity type and API version. In the published Australian requirements, an accurately classified individual account may be verified from personal identity, contact, bank and activity details without an ABN appearing as a standard required field, while an unincorporated organisation or non-profit generally needs business tax-identification information. Stripe separately says an ABN is typically required when verifying an Australian unincorporated entity.

The connected status of this account shows that onboarding was completed; it does not reveal which entity classification or evidence Stripe accepted, and it is not a ruling on ABN entitlement. Record the dashboard's actual classification privately and correct it if it does not match reality.

There is also a separate processor-contract issue: Stripe's Australian Services Agreement says its services are for business purposes and not personal, family or household use. That contractual eligibility rule is not the same question as whether Australian law requires or permits an ABN. A genuine creator activity may be a business purpose for Stripe while its tax and ABN classification still depends on the Australian facts.

The structure must match reality. Do not select “individual” merely to avoid an ABN if the activity has become a sole-trader business. ABN entitlement turns on whether the person is carrying on or starting an enterprise. Relevant indicators include a profit intention, repetition, commercial scale and business-like organisation. Free software and an informal “not for profit” description do not settle that classification.

On the current facts, public branding, an organised launch and repeated online support create some business indicators, while free access, no commercial sale and no promised consideration point the other way. The conservative operational position is to keep complete records and treat the receipts as potentially assessable from the first payment. If certainty is needed before the facts develop, obtain an accountant's advice or an ATO private ruling.

If the activity is a business and is carried on publicly under `Eclipse`, `Umbra` or `Eclipse/Umbra` instead of the maintainer's exact personal name, ASIC business-name registration will generally be required and needs an ABN or pending ABN application. The applications themselves can still be called Eclipse and Umbra when the individual recipient is accurately identified.

An ABN record is public. The ABR publishes the ABN, legal/entity name, entity type, state/postcode and registered business names; this privacy consequence should be understood before applying.

## Australian tax and records

Calling a payment a “tip”, “support” or “donation” does not decide its income-tax treatment. Buy Me a Coffee says support is typically subject to income tax. ATO guidance likewise distinguishes a personal gift from a receipt connected with work, services, a business or another income-producing activity. Public payments solicited because of the software work are not equivalent to family or birthday gifts.

If the activity is treated as a sole-trader business:

- report it in the individual's tax return; there is no separate sole-trader tax return;
- record the **gross support amount**, not only the net bank payout;
- record Buy Me a Coffee and Stripe fees, refunds, chargebacks and eligible project expenses separately; and
- convert foreign-currency amounts to AUD using an allowable and consistently applied method.

If the activity remains a genuine hobby, hobby receipts are generally not declared as business income and hobby losses are not deductible. Do not mix those positions by declaring only profitable receipts while claiming hobby costs as business deductions. “Other income” versus business-income treatment is fact-specific even when declaring conservatively.

Income tax and GST are separate. A genuinely reward-free payment is likely not consideration for a supply where the supporter receives no goods, services, access, credit placement, voting right, promised feature or development commitment. Adding any of those benefits can change the GST analysis. If the person carries on an enterprise and current or projected GST turnover reaches A$75,000, GST registration is generally required within 21 days and an ABN is required first. Do not use the A$150,000 non-profit-body threshold merely because the software is free.

Keep transaction exports, fee statements, refunds, disputes, payout reports, original currency, AUD conversion method, bank entries and a dated copy of the public offer from the first payment. Business tax records generally need to be kept for at least five years. Retain dispute evidence longer where necessary.

## Other Australian considerations

- The recipient is not an Australian deductible gift recipient. Do not call support tax deductible, issue a DGR receipt, or present the project or individual as a charity.
- Queensland's `Collections Act 1966` can regulate public appeals for a charitable or Minister-determined community purpose. Whether the current page itself is a section 5(2) community-purpose appeal is unresolved, and the Act assigns that determination to the Minister; discretionary personal-tip wording and the absence of a ring-fenced fund are not a legal safe harbour. Do not claim charity, DGR status or a project/community-purpose fund. A written Queensland OFT classification remains the clearest route to certainty before broad public promotion and is required before changing to an expressly earmarked model.
- Buy Me a Coffee and Stripe will process supporter and recipient data and may transfer or store it overseas. Buy Me a Coffee's terms say a creator may receive supporter information including names, email addresses and postal addresses; a supporter may also submit a display name or message. Keep that data on the platforms where practical, export only what is needed for support, disputes or compliance, and never reuse it for marketing without separate express consent and a working unsubscribe route.
- One-time and monthly support already paid is ordinarily non-refundable under Buy Me a Coffee's policy, but duplicate, unauthorised and individually reviewed payments can still be refunded. Monthly cancellation stops future renewals. Chargebacks can remove the payment and add a dispute fee. Keep a small reserve rather than treating every new payment as immediately final.
- Stripe treats creator-tip platforms as restricted at the platform level but states that individual creators using an approved platform do not need pre-approval. Do not present this account as charitable fundraising or enable prohibited/restricted rewards without rechecking the current rules.
- Buy Me a Coffee's terms incorporate Stripe's terms, use United States law, require claims relating to the service to begin within one year, include a broad indemnity for breaches or legal violations, and allow service changes or account action. Its current terms also allow an account/page to be treated as dormant after 12 months of inactivity and permit restrictions, closure, refunds or other handling of an unwithdrawn balance. Log in and reconcile the account regularly; do not leave a balance unattended.

## Live configuration and maintenance

The page is active, so the remaining items are record-keeping and profile-maintenance tasks rather than pre-link gates:

1. Keep the canonical creator page at `https://buymeacoffee.com/preceptorofmagic` connected to the recipient's accurately classified Australian Stripe account and bank account.
2. Record the Stripe dashboard's actual entity classification and any ABN supplied. If the facts or onboarding require a sole-trader or other structure, obtain the correct ABN and resolve any ASIC business-name requirement; never invent or borrow an identifier.
3. Keep support reward-free. One-time and monthly support are enabled; memberships, Shop, commissions, rewards and supporter-only content remain off.
4. Record the USD base currency, localised-pricing setting, who absorbs card fees, refund/contact route and the actual fee/FX treatment from the first real transaction.
5. After the first payout review, archive the review result, gross receipt, fees, conversion, payout and bank entry. Do not treat completion of a review that requires prior support as an activation gate.
6. Keep the verified username as the sole active line in `.github/FUNDING.yml`:

   ```yaml
   buy_me_a_coffee: PreceptorOfMagic
   ```

7. Keep one semantic HTTPS link to `https://buymeacoffee.com/preceptorofmagic` instead of loading the provider's third-party button script on every page. Keep the recipient, purpose, fees, currency, recurring status, cancellation, refund, privacy, tax, contact and closure disclosures beside it.
8. Before the project itself becomes public, replace the generic creator biography with a project-specific description and, for visual consistency, change the public Buy Me a Coffee page colour from its current blue `#5F7FFF` to the site's purple `#BD5FFF` if that option remains available.
9. Check the Buy Me a Coffee page, GitHub sponsor menu and site while signed out. Ensure no repository, organisation-default, manifest or inherited funding entry points to another recipient.
